ACE Methodology

Direction first.
Deployment second.

Four stages, in order. Each one has to earn the next: we do not build until the value case holds and the controls are designed.

  1. 01 / DIAGNOSTIC

    Map how the firm actually works

    Workflow by workflow: where the hours go, how many handoffs a task crosses, which steps are still manual, and where the data lives. The output is an operating picture of the firm, not a technology survey.

    • Origination4 handoffs
    • Screening3 systems
    • DiligenceManual
    • IC preparation6 handoffs
    • Portfolio monitoring30–45 day lag
    • LP reportingRebuilt monthly

    Illustrative — a diagnostic returns your firm’s own figures

  2. 02 / AI OPPORTUNITY

    Rank by economic value, not by what is possible

    Every candidate workflow is scored on impact, feasibility, risk and time-to-value, then sequenced. Most of the work at this stage is deciding what not to build.

    WorkflowImpactFeasibilityTime‑to‑value
    DiligenceHighHigh30 days
    IC preparationHighHigh21 days
    LP reportingMediumHigh45 days

    Illustrative prioritisation — sequencing comes out of the diagnostic

  3. 03 / BLUEPRINT

    Design the architecture, including who signs off

    What connects to what, which data is reachable and by whom, where a person reviews before anything leaves the system, and how the result will be measured once live.

    Firm dataCRMSharePointDriveData roomEmailFinancials
    ACE intelligence layerKnowledgeAgentsWorkflowsPermissions
    Human reviewInvestment teamOperating partnerCFO
    Institutional outputIC memoDiligence packKPI dashboardLP report

  4. 04 / DEPLOYMENT

    Institutional work product, produced in the open

    The system stops being a project when the firm's actual deliverables start coming out of it. Every artefact carries the inputs it was built from, so it can be checked.

    • IC memoFrom the deal record
    • Diligence packFrom the data room
    • Portfolio KPI reportFrom portfolio data
    • Target listFrom the buy-box
    • Board packFrom the operating model

Assurance

Controls belong inside the system.

Permissions, review and traceability are part of the deployment architecture. They are designed at blueprint stage, not added as a compliance layer once the system is already running.

  1. 01DataScoped per workflow, from sources the firm already controls
  2. 02WorkflowPrepared, ranked, drafted, monitored
  3. 03Review gateExceptions surfaced; nothing consequential passes silently
  4. 04DecisionInvestment judgment stays with your team
  5. 05OutputTraceable back to the inputs that produced it

Role-based permissions

Access follows the firm's existing structure. A deployment does not widen who can reach a document.

Human review gates

Placed during design. Consequential output does not leave the system without an approval.

Auditability

Who ran what, when, and against which inputs, recorded as the system operates.

Model and data isolation

Firm data is kept to the deployment it belongs to and is not pooled across clients.

Encryption

In transit and at rest, using the providers and key handling agreed for the engagement.

Data retention

A design decision made at blueprint stage, not a setting discovered after go-live.

Source traceability

Generated work carries its sources, so a reviewer can check a claim rather than trust it.

Deployment controls

Named people in your firm can pause, change or withdraw any workflow without going through us.

Controls are specified per engagement and written into the contract. ACE states certification status plainly rather than implying it, and where a firm requires a named standard we scope to it before work begins.

  • SOC 2 — not currently held
  • ISO 27001 — not currently held
  • Data processing terms — per engagement
